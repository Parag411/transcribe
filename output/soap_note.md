# SOAP Note

## S — Subjective

**Chief Complaint:** Pain on the outside of the right elbow for several months, progressively worsening.

**History of Present Illness:** Patient reports lateral right elbow pain onset several months ago with no trauma or trigger. Pain is achy and sometimes sharp (4/10), worsened by wrist flexion/extension and lifting heavy objects at work (repetitive assembly line work). Resting and ibuprofen provide relief. OTC topical ointment also helped. Pain is progressive and starting to affect work performance.

**Pain Scale:** 4/10

**Past Medical History:** C-section approximately 17 years ago (non-emergency). Otherwise healthy with no chronic conditions.

**Current Medications:** Tylenol (acetaminophen) and Advil (ibuprofen) PRN for pain.

**Allergies:** No known drug allergies.

**Review of Systems:** Denies: weight loss, fever/chills, fatigue, rashes, sensory changes (vision/hearing/smell/taste), cough, shortness of breath, chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, nausea/vomiting, bowel/urinary changes, memory loss, numbness/tingling, radiation of pain, cold sensation in arm. Full ROM of right elbow and hand maintained. Pain isolated to lateral right elbow.


## O — Objective

**Vital Signs:** Not documented.

**Physical Examination:** Right elbow: No redness, swelling, skin changes, or deformities. No asymmetry vs left elbow. No warmth on palpation. Tenderness on palpation of lateral epicondyle. No tenderness on medial aspect, forearm, or biceps. Pain reproduced with wrist extension and wrist flexion with elbow in full extension. No pain with elbow flexion, shoulder extension, shoulder adduction, forearm pronation or supination.

**Diagnostic Results:** No imaging ordered. Clinician determined imaging not necessary given absence of trauma and low concern for fracture.


## A — Assessment

**Primary Diagnosis:** Lateral epicondylitis (Tennis Elbow) — right elbow.

**Differential Diagnosis:** Radial tunnel syndrome, posterior interosseous nerve entrapment, elbow osteoarthritis.


## P — Plan

**Treatment:** 1. Activity modification: avoid repetitive wrist extensor movements at work. 2. Ice application for pain and swelling. 3. Compression sleeve. 4. Physical therapy to improve mobility and strength in right arm.

**Medications Prescribed:** Continue Tylenol (acetaminophen) and NSAIDs (ibuprofen/Advil) PRN for pain.

**Follow Up:** Expected resolution in a few months with conservative management; may take up to 6 months to 2 years. Consider splinting if symptoms persist.

**Patient Education:** Explained diagnosis of lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow). Likely caused by repetitive strain and continuous contraction of wrist extensor muscle group at work. Reassured no fracture concern and no imaging required. Advised on activity modification, compression sleeve, ice therapy, and physical therapy.
